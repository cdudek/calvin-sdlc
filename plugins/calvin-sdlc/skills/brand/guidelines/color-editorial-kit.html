Binding palette for everything designed outside the product. Only these eleven values. Carry no colour over from the web and product world. Always output colours as hex, token names only as a supplement: external tools, Canva, Figma and AI image generators cannot resolve token names.

Do

  • Output hex values directly, token name only in brackets after them.
  • One accent per visual. Strong tone always on its own light counterpart.
  • Build charts from the medium and light tones, main series in strong.
  • Check contrast: body copy at least 4.5:1.

Don't

  • No bg-surface, text-main, border-main or a status colour into a slide, PDF or ad asset.
  • No #7337fc or #601ee3 from the UI brand palette. Purple here is #8259ff.
  • No in-between tones, no opacity steps as a substitute for the light tones.
  • No gradients unless the user explicitly asks for them.

Exception 1 from colour world 1 — surface backgrounds

These two tones may be used here as well. Exclusively as box and surface backgrounds: callouts, cards, table rows, quote boxes. Not for text, icons, lines, chart series or full-bleed backgrounds. This is not a general permeability between the colour worlds.

Exception 2 from colour world 1 — outlines, dividers, tables

For outlines, dividers, tables and comparable structural elements in editorial these neutral tones from the UI kit may be used. They secure legibility and thus better UX in papers and documents too. Not for headlines, surface colours, accents or chart series. Text colour stays #000000 or #ffffff.
