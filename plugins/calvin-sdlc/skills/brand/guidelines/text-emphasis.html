Text emphasis

For ad and social-media visuals and all non-website assets. There are exactly three kinds of emphasis, each with a fixed place of use. Add nothing, mix nothing.

HEADLINE → highlight bar or outline type  ·  BODY COPY → bold  ·  bar & outline only in capitals (no mixed case)  ·  never bar/outline in body copy, never bold in headlines

No fixed pixel values, no clearance value, no slack calculation,
no negative margins, no offset, no second compensation.
  • Only in capitals (continuous capitals), never in mixed case.
  • Headlines are always left-aligned with a ragged right edge. Never right-aligned, never centred, never justified, not even for headlines with a bar (CLAUDE.md §11).
  • Principle: the bar height is not derived from the line height. It is the cap block plus a set inner padding, equal above and below: 0.745 + 2 × 0.2025 = 1.15em. text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic trims the box to cap height and baseline, after which the padding is exactly the measured distance.
  • Exactly one vertical rule. No clearance value, no slack deduction, no offset, no line-spacing allowance, no second compensation. Earlier vertical corrections are removed, not adjusted.
  • All dimensions come from tokens/highlight.css, the leading source is OMR_Highlightbalken.fig. Cards and assets define no values of their own.
  • The vertical inner padding is a target value, not a result: 0.2025em from the cap top to the bar top and 0.2025em from the baseline to the bar bottom. Both values are exactly equal. At the sides there are 0.159em padding plus roughly 0.05em glyph side bearing, so roughly 0.21em — the inner padding is thus consistent on all sides. Do not readjust.
  • Centred on the cap block: the same distance above and below. The line box centres on ascender plus descender and would thus sit structurally 0.022em too low; that is why the height is set on the cap block, not on the line height.
  • Line spacing: none. The lines abut with spacing 0. Because the bar box is 1.15em low and the line box 1.353em high, between two bars on neighbouring lines there automatically remain 0.203em. This value is not set, it falls out.
  • Diacritics: if the bar text contains a character above cap height (Ä Ö Ü, accents), the bar gets 0.127em extra padding at the top — bar height 1.277em. The 0.2025em at the bottom and everything at the sides stay unchanged. The dots reach up to 0.996em and thus sit 0.0755em below the bar top. The larger bar height stays below the line height of 1.353em; even with umlauts the line spacing is not increased.
  • Word spacing: The reference value of the .fig is 0.30em between the bar edge and the neighbouring word, in addition to the inner padding. For that the bar carries no outer margin: any inline margin on the bar also acts at the start of the line and breaks the left text edge there. Rendered, the space in the markup delivers 0.19em. The difference is open and is not closed by a compensation on the bar.
  • Scaling: all values are multiples of the font size and scale with it. No fixed pixel values. The earlier minimum distance of 5 px is gone.
  • The stroke weight is the measured stem width of the font file in the respective cut; for the bar OMR One Extrabold (800) = 0.171em. The earlier value 0.11em is wrong and deleted.
  • Setting: the bar sits on the most important word or on the main statement of the headline. One bar per word or per coherent core statement.
  • Wrapping the bar statement is permitted. If it wraps, two independent barsresult, one per line; no continuous bar, no shared box across two lines. Each carries the full geometry (0.2025em above and below, 0.159em at the sides, anchored to the cap block), the diacritics allowance only where diacritics occur. A bar with an umlaut is thus taller than its partner. That is intended.
  • Between the two bars the same distance applies as between two bars on neighbouring lines: it falls out as 0.203em from line height minus bar height and is not set. The bars never touch.
  • The break happens at a word boundary. A word is never split, neither with nor without a hyphen. Both bars start on the left text edge, unless text precedes them; no indent, no offset. At most two lines per bar statement; if three would be needed, shorten to the core word.
  • To wrap or not , checked in this order: if the whole statement fits into the current line, it stays there. If it does not fit, the break is set inside the statement as soon as pulling it closed would leave a visible gap at the end of the previous line. If the previous line stays filled even without the bar, the statement moves to the next line as a whole. A gap at the line end that is clearly wider than one word of the headline is not acceptable.
  • One bar colour per visual, also with two bars from one statement.
  • Bars on several lines are permitted and never touch under this geometry.
  • Bar as display:inline-block with line-height:1, text-box:trim-both cap alphabetic and white-space:nowrap.
  • Rectangle, square corners — no rounding, no shadow, no gradient.
  • One single bar colour per visual. The colour follows the background per CLAUDE.md §7.5: on black #8259ff or #ffffff, on purple, white and all light tones #000000. Coloured bars belong on black or white. On title slides and chapter dividers §6 applies.
  • Only for single important words in a headline, never in body copy, never across the board.

  • Only in capitals (continuous capitals), never in mixed case.
  • Headlines are always left-aligned with a ragged right edge. Never right-aligned, never centred, never justified, not even for headlines with a bar (CLAUDE.md §11).
  • Fill transparent set and at the same time a visible outline (stroke) give it; both together.
  • Removing only the fill makes the text invisible — that is always wrong.
  • Stroke colour follows the contrast pairing (white outline on purple/black, black outline on light).
  • Stroke weight strong enough for legibility, but not so thick that the letterform closes up.
KIND 3

Bold

only in body copy · OMR One Bold (700)

In body copy single words are emphasised with OMR One Bold (700) . The text stays calmly legible and the important terms still stand out, without bar or outline.

  • Exclusively in body/running text, never in headlines.
  • Always implemented via the cut OMR One Bold (700).
  • In body copy no highlight bars and no outline type.
