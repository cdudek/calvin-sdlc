# 04 Lockup and spacing

Screen: png/04-lockup-and-spacing.png

[image: heydiff]

**04 / LOCKUP AND SPACING**

## Lockup 5:4 and spacing

Every measure comes from the logo itself. u is one fifth of the mark height, e is the height of the lowercase e. Both scale with the logo, so the rules hold at any size.

[image: Lockup construction with mark 5u, wordmark 4u, gap 1u and clear space of 1e]

**MARK**

#### 5u

One fifth of the mark height is the unit u.

**WORDMARK**

#### 4u

Top of the h to the foot of the y, centred on the mark.

**GAP**

#### 1u

One unit, a fifth of the mark height. Picked by eye over 0.85u and 1.15u.

**CLEAR SPACE**

#### 1e

The height of the e on every side, about 45 percent of the mark height. Same distance for the mark on its own. More is fine. App icons and avatars are the exception: the icon edge is the clear space.

**MINIMUM SIZE**

#### 24 px

Lockup 24 px tall, about 92 px wide. Mark on its own 16 px.

#### Before it goes out

Place the supplied files

Never rebuild the lockup from the mark and a typed word. The files on page 35 already hold the 5:4 ratio and the 1u gap.

Check clear space with the e

Hold the height of the e against every side. Nothing else enters that space, not even a hairline.

Below 24 px, the mark alone

Under 24 px the wordmark stops reading. Use the mark, flat, from 16 px.

Trust the eye on dark

Light shapes on dark look heavier. Use the reverse artwork, which is already corrected.

**WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM**

The ratio and gap tests and the golden ratio research are in the appendix, A1 and A3.

# A3 Appendix / Golden ratio research

Screen: png/a3-appendix-golden-ratio-research.png

[image: heydiff]

**A3 / APPENDIX / GOLDEN RATIO**

## Golden ratio research

Background for the lockup and spacing rules on page 04. Kept for reference; nothing here is a rule.

#### What the research says about the golden ratio

One study supports it, with a catch

Pittard, Ewing and Jevons (2007) tested logos in Australia, Singapore and South Africa. People preferred 1.618 for logos based on natural forms, but 1:1 for geometric, constructed logos.

Famous examples are fitted after the fact

Measured closely, the Apple logo does not follow the ratio (Fast Company, 2013). Twitter described its 2012 bird as built from overlapping circles, with no mention of it.

Mathematicians push back

Markowsky (1992) is the classic critique of overstated golden ratio claims in art and design.

Brand teams measure with the logo

Scale defines clear space as the height of its e, AAB uses the height of the logo. Anyone can check it by eye.

The eye wins over the grid

Round shapes need to overshoot, light shapes on dark look heavier. Grids guide, the eye decides.

**HOW WE USE IT**

1.618 is a starting point, not a rule. A golden lockup would be 5 : 3.1, close to the 5:3 test. We keep 5:4 because it reads better at small sizes. For the gap, 1.618 gave 0.85u, but a full unit looked better side by side, so the gap is 1u.

#### Sources

Pittard, Ewing and Jevons (2007), Aesthetic theory and logo design, International Journal of Market Research.

Fast Company (2013), The Apple logo and the golden ratio.

Twitter design blog (2012), the new bird built from overlapping circles.

Markowsky (1992), Misconceptions about the golden ratio, The College Mathematics Journal.
