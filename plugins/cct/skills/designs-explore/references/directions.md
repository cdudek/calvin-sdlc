# The direction catalogue

Three directions per run. Pick a **spread**, then paste its three rows verbatim into the Codex
prompt, one row per run.

A spread is the axis along which the three differ. Picking one axis and holding everything else
still is what makes the comparison mean something — three designs that differ on five axes at once
cannot be compared, only preferred.

## Spread A — risk (the default)

Use when nobody has decided anything yet and the point is to find the edges.

| # | Slug | The direction, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `safe` | **Safe.** The version a competitor would ship and nobody would question. Conventional layout, familiar patterns, no surprises. Your job is to make the obvious version genuinely good, not to make it boring — this is the benchmark the other two have to beat. |
| 02 | `bold` | **Bold.** One strong idea, pushed all the way. Pick the single thing this screen is for and make it dominate — scale, position, contrast, whitespace. Cut or demote everything that competes with it. A reviewer should be able to name the idea in one sentence. |
| 03 | `contrarian` | **Contrarian.** Question the brief's own shape. If it asks for a dashboard, ask whether it should be a feed. If it asks for a form, ask whether it should be a conversation. Change the interaction model, not the paint. You may be wrong; be wrong in an interesting way. |

## Spread B — density

Use when the content is known and the argument is about how much to show at once.

| # | Slug | The direction, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `editorial` | **Editorial.** Generous whitespace, one thing at a time, strong typographic hierarchy. Optimised for a first-time reader who needs to understand before acting. |
| 02 | `balanced` | **Balanced.** The working default: scannable, moderate density, secondary detail one interaction away. Optimised for someone who uses this weekly. |
| 03 | `dense` | **Dense.** Maximum information per screen without becoming unreadable. Tables over cards, tight rhythm, every pixel earning its place. Optimised for someone who lives in this all day. |

## Spread C — one direction, three takes

Use when the direction is already agreed and the argument is about execution.

| # | Slug | The direction, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `take-structure` | Hold the agreed direction. Vary the **structure**: what is grouped with what, what is primary, what order the eye takes. |
| 02 | `take-rhythm` | Hold the agreed direction. Vary the **rhythm**: spacing scale, type scale, how much air between blocks, where the page breathes. |
| 03 | `take-emphasis` | Hold the agreed direction. Vary the **emphasis**: which single element carries the weight, and how the rest steps back. |

## Spread D — entry point

Use for a flow, when the argument is where the user starts.

| # | Slug | The direction, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `guided` | **Guided.** One decision per step, explicit progress, no way to get lost. Longer, safer. |
| 02 | `single-surface` | **Single surface.** Everything on one screen, progressive disclosure instead of steps. Faster for people who know what they want. |
| 03 | `smart-default` | **Smart default.** Start from a filled-in, sensible answer and let the user correct it, rather than asking them to build it from nothing. |

## Writing your own spread

The rule: **one axis, three positions on it, and the middle one has to be a real option.** A spread
whose middle is a compromise nobody wants gives you a two-way choice dressed up as three.

Say the axis out loud in `brief.md`, so the reviewer knows what they are being asked to decide.
